🚦 Traffic Jams vs Factories: Who Pollutes More?

Have you ever been stuck in a traffic jam and wondered how much pollution all those vehicles are producing? Or is it actually factories that are responsible for most of the pollution?

That simple question became the idea behind this project.

I wanted to use real data instead of assumptions to understand whether traffic-related emissions or industrial activities have a stronger relationship with air pollution in India.

---

🎯 What I Wanted to Find Out

The main question of this project is:

«Does traffic congestion contribute more to air pollution than factories?»

To explore this, I looked at different environmental and transportation indicators such as:

- 🌫️ AQI (Air Quality Index)
- 🏭 Industrial/Factory activity
- 🚗 Vehicle & traffic-related data
- PM2.5
- PM10
- 📍 Location-wise pollution
- 📅 Year-wise trends

---

📊 What I Did

I collected and cleaned the datasets, prepared them for analysis, and then used Microsoft Excel to explore the relationships between traffic, industrial activity, and air quality.

The analysis includes:

- Data cleaning and preparation
- Removing inconsistencies and handling missing data
- Trend analysis
- Comparative analysis
- Pivot Tables
- KPIs
- Charts & visualizations
- Interactive dashboard
- Final insights based on the data

---

---

💡 Why This Project?

Air pollution is something we experience almost every day, especially in large cities.

Instead of simply saying "factories cause pollution" or "vehicles cause pollution," I wanted to approach the problem from a data perspective.

The goal wasn't to prove one side right from the beginning.

The goal was to let the data tell the story.

---

📈 Dashboard

The final Excel dashboard brings the analysis together in one place, making it easier to compare pollution levels, identify trends, and understand the relationship between traffic, industries, and air quality.

---

🔍 Key Takeaway

This project helped me understand that real-world data is rarely as simple as choosing one single cause.

Air pollution can be influenced by multiple factors, and the relationship can vary depending on location, time, weather, traffic conditions, and industrial activity.

That's what made this project interesting for me—not just creating charts, but using data to investigate a real-world question.
